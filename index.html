Sketch Narrative - Bar Chart Clock

This sketch explores representing time using a horizontal bar chart rather than a traditional clock. The visualization is intended for students or people working in timed sessions, similar to a Pomodoro timer, where it is useful to quickly understand how much time has passed during a task.

  • Context: This clock is designed for studying or focused work sessions. Instead of reading numbers or clock hands, users can quickly see the passage of time through the length of bars representing hours and minutes.
  • Design decisions: A horizontal bar chart was chosen because bar length is a clear visual encoding for progress. Each bar represents a unit of time, and the increasing length communicates how much time has passed. Distinct colors were used to help differentiate the time units and improve readability.
  • Future work: In future versions, the bars will update dynamically using the hour(), minute(), and second() functions so the chart reflects the current time. Additional improvements could include animation or interaction such as hovering over bars to reveal the exact time.

HWK 5 – Narrative Design Note

Dataset & Story: This visualization compares U.S. unemployment rate and fitness industry employment from 2020–2024. The story highlights how sector-specific employment responds differently to macroeconomic shifts.

Author-Driven or User-Driven: This is primarily author-driven with light interaction via a year slider. The slider allows exploration, but the visual metaphor frames the interpretation.

Visualization Genre: Metaphorical narrative visualization with interactive filtering.

Design Principles: Clear visual hierarchy (title → numbers → icons), consistent encoding, limited annotation, and ethical scaling using labeled values.
